HR Employee Attrition Analysis

Project Overview

This project analyses employee attrition using data analytics tools to understand why employees leave an organisation and identify workforce trends.

The project uses Excel, MySQL, Python and Power BI to clean, analyse and visualise HR data.

Business Problem

Employee attrition can increase recruitment costs, reduce productivity and affect organisational performance.

The objective of this project is to analyse employee information, identify attrition patterns and provide data-driven recommendations to support better HR decisions.

Project Objectives

- Analyse employee attrition rates.
- Identify departments and job roles with higher attrition.
- Understand the relationship between overtime, satisfaction and attrition.
- Analyse employee age, income and work experience.
- Build an interactive HR analytics dashboard.
- Provide actionable business recommendations.

Dataset Description

Dataset: HR Employee Attrition

- Total records: 1,470 employees
- Total original columns: 35
- Missing values: 0
- Duplicate records: 0

The dataset contains employee demographics, department, job role, monthly income, job satisfaction, overtime and attrition information.

Key Business Insights

- Overall employee attrition is 16.12%.
- Sales has the highest department attrition rate at approximately 20.63%.
- Human Resources has an attrition rate of approximately 19.05%.
- Research and Development has an attrition rate of approximately 13.84%.
- Sales Representatives have a particularly high attrition rate.
- Employees aged 25–34 account for the largest number of employees who left.

Business Recommendations

- Investigate the reasons behind higher attrition in the Sales department.
- Review employee workload and overtime practices.
- Improve employee engagement and job satisfaction.
- Develop career growth and promotion opportunities.
- Monitor attrition across departments and job roles.
- Use regular HR analytics reporting to support workforce planning.

Project Limitations

- The dataset does not contain a suitable date column for time-based analysis.
- The dataset provides limited information about the reasons employees leave.
- The analysis identifies associations and patterns but does not establish causation.

Conclusion

This project demonstrates how multiple data analytics tools can be integrated to analyse HR employee attrition.

The findings provide useful insights into employee turnover, workforce distribution and organisational patterns. The interactive Power BI dashboard helps HR teams explore the data and support informed decision-making.
